# Phase XV — Korobov–Vinogradov moving-exponent sign stability

**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** rigorous consequence of a classical published Mertens-function estimate and the Phase XII finite certificate. Not peer-reviewed; no independent novelty claim; **not a solution of the Riemann Hypothesis**.

## Definitions and source inputs

Let \(\mu(n)\) be the Möbius function, \(M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)\), and
\[
 a_n=\mu(n)M(n-1),\qquad C_\alpha(N)=\sum_{n=2}^N a_n n^{-\alpha}.
\]

**Classical external theorem:** There are constants \(A>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(x_0\) such that for \(x\ge x_0\),
\[
|M(x)|\le A x\exp\!\left[-\kappa\,\frac{(\log x)^{3/5}}{(\log\log x)^{1/5}}\right]. \tag{KV}
\]
This follows from the Korobov–Vinogradov zero-free region via Perron-type estimates. This repository does not independently establish (KV) or give a numerical value for \(\kappa\). **It is an unconditional theorem from prior research, not new RH progress.** Sources listed below.

**Prior finite certificate:** Phase XII's C++ certificate plus its separately invoked explicit Mertens bound establish \(C_2(N)<-1/10\) for all integers \(N\ge2\), so \(C_2(\infty)\le-1/10\), with convergence secured by (KV) or the explicit logarithmic bound. That bound is a prior conclusion used here, not regenerated by the present numerical experiment.

## Theorem (near-2 stability with Korobov–Vinogradov input)

Suppose \(\delta_N\ge0\) and
\[
\delta_N=o\!\left((\log N)^{-2/5}(\log\log N)^{-1/5}\right).\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{2-\delta_N}(N)=C_2(\infty)<-1/10.}\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, *uniformly* over real \(\alpha\) in the interval \([2-\delta_N,2]\),
\[
\boxed{\sup_{2-\delta_N\le\alpha\le2}|C_\alpha(N)-C_2(\infty)|\to0.}\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, \(C_\alpha(N)<-1/20\) for every \(\alpha\in[2-\delta_N,2]\) once \(N\) is sufficiently large. In particular, \(\delta_N=1/\sqrt{\log N}\) satisfies (1).

### Proof

Define \(F(t)=t^{3/5}/(\log t)^{1/5}\) for \(t>e\). Since \(\log(n-1)/\log n\to1\), the classical input (KV) yields constants \(B,b>0\) and an integer \(n_0\) such that
\[
\frac{|a_n|}{n^2}\le\frac Bn e^{-bF(\log n)}\qquad (n\ge n_0).\tag{4}
\]
The lower cutoff \(n_0\) can be taken large enough that \(\log n_0>e\).

Put \(L=\log N\). Since \(F(L)/L=L^{-2/5}(\log L)^{-1/5}\), hypothesis (1) implies \(\delta_N\to0\) and, eventually,
\[
0\le\delta_N\le (b/2)F(L)/L.\tag{5}
\]
The function \(F(t)/t=t^{-2/5}(\log t)^{-1/5}\) is decreasing for \(t>e\). Thus, for \(\log n_0\le t\le L\),
\[
\delta_N t\le\frac b2\frac{F(L)}L t\le\frac b2 F(t).\tag{6}
\]
Equations (4)–(6) imply a common summable majorant, for all sufficiently large \(N\) and \(n_0\le n\le N\):
\[
\frac{|a_n|}{n^{2-\delta_N}}\le\frac Bn\exp[-\tfrac b2 F(\log n)].\tag{7}
\]
The right-hand side is summable: the integral test and \(t=\log x\) reduce convergence to that of \(\int^\infty e^{-(b/2)F(t)}dt\). Since \(F(t)\ge \sqrt t\) eventually, the latter integral converges.

For each fixed integer \(n\), \(\delta_N\to0\), hence
\(n^{\delta_N}-1\to0\). By dominated convergence for series, (4)–(7) imply
\[
\sum_{n=2}^N\frac{|a_n|}{n^2}(n^{\delta_N}-1)\longrightarrow0.\tag{8}
\]
For every \(0\le\delta\le\delta_N\), the left-hand side of
\[
|C_{2-\delta}(N)-C_2(N)|\le \sum_{n=2}^N\frac{|a_n|}{n^2}(n^{\delta_N}-1)\tag{9}
\]
is bounded by the same quantity tending to zero. Meanwhile, the series at exponent 2 converges absolutely by (4). Thus (3) holds. The Phase XII negative upper bound then yields (2) and eventual uniform negativity.

\(\square\)

## Quantitative comparison and limitations

Phase XIV's logarithmic estimate permitted \(\delta_N=c(\log\log N)/\log N\) for \(0<c<1\). The new result covers any \(\delta_N=o((\log N)^{-2/5}(\log\log N)^{-1/5})\), a much wider moving window. For example \(\delta_N=(\log N)^{-1/2}\) is permitted.

**Why this does not give a fixed exponent:** For every fixed \(\delta>0\), the positive exponential \(n^\delta=\exp(\delta\log n)\) ultimately dominates the unconditional suppression \(\exp[-bF(\log n)]\), because \(F(t)=o(t)\). Thus the absolute-value majorant (7) cannot be extended to fixed \(\alpha=2-\delta\). This observation is a limitation of the present proof technique, **not a theorem forbidding other signed methods**.

In the region \(\alpha<2\), a fixed-\(\alpha\) uniform upper bound on \(C_\alpha(N)\) would imply a new zero-free half-plane for \(\zeta(s)\); nothing of that kind follows from the moving-exponent theorem.

## Reproduction

The attached program `experiments/riemann_phase15_korobov_corridor.cpp` uses an independently cross-checked sieve (trial-division Möbius values through 2000) and evaluates the **finite** sums at several checkpoints, with \(\delta_N=1/\sqrt{\log N}\).

```bash
g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -Wall -Wextra experiments/riemann_phase15_korobov_corridor.cpp -o phase15
./phase15
```

Numerical values from this program are illustrative and do **not** certify the asymptotic result. The theorem uses the external KV bound and Phase XII's finite proof certificate.
